## Problem Statement

When London transit is disrupted, rideshare demand near the affected stations jumps. Uber detects this from ride requests and surges its prices (+90–150% has been reported on Tube strike days) and uses that surge to pull extra drivers in. A fixed-fare minicab firm *should* win the riders who are priced out by that surge. Instead it loses them: it has no early warning, so its drivers aren't nearby, and riders don't think of it at that moment.

SurgeSignal turns public signals (TfL disruption status, weather, major events, time of day) into **specific actions for a minicab dispatcher**: "move 2 idle cars toward Clapham North / Clapham South / Stockwell". **Hypothesis to test:** the alert arrives minutes before the spike shows up in the firm's own calls and bookings (see Assignment Q6).

**How repositioning creates value (legal constraint):** London private-hire vehicles must be pre-booked. They can't take street hails or wait at stations for passengers. The benefit comes from **shorter pickup times on app and phone bookings from the affected area**. Cars already nearby mean the firm can quote a 5-min ETA instead of 20, accept more jobs, and turn fewer away to Uber. Drivers wait nearby, legally parked, **not at the station**.

Pitch line: *Uber's surge is your opening. Be there first, at a fixed fare.*

## Demand Evidence

- **One conversation with a London private-hire operator.** Paraphrase: they feel Uber's surge gives Uber an unfair advantage and they are losing out. When asked what they lose, it was **the customers**, not drivers and not positioning speed.
- The firm, the operator's role, fleet size, area and booking channel were **not** captured. This is the biggest evidence gap. The Assignment closes it, and the answers gate Phase B (see Recommended Approach).
- External evidence: a study of unplanned rail disruptions in Chicago (CTA, 2018–19) found ride-hailing responds to disruptions, and ride-hailing uplift near disrupted lines has been reported at about +30%. **Assumption:** that this transfers to London. Check the study's exact figure before citing it (arXiv 2202.09466). On a Tube strike day, Uber had **2× normal cars online** while **Addison Lee was fully booked with 85-min waits** (Time Out, Uber UK blog).

## Status Quo

The operator's firm **does nothing proactive**. It finds out about disruptions when calls spike and reacts after the fact. The key consequence is that the riders it loses to Uber never call, so the firm **can't see its own losses**. Existing dispatch software (iCabbi, Cab9, TaxiCaller, Magenta Echo) handles bookings and live capacity. Only Echo forecasts shortages, and it forecasts only from bookings already in the system, never from external signals like TfL.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **User:** the person at a small/mid London minicab firm who decides where idle drivers wait. Whether that's an owner-dispatcher or a shift dispatcher is **unknown** (Assignment Q1).
- **Wedge:** a Telegram alert to that person during **unplanned, local disruptions**: signal failures, station closures, part-line suspensions. That's where minutes of lead time matter, the spike is short and local, and the firm has spare drivers.
- **Why not lead with strikes:** strikes are announced days ahead, so no prediction is needed, and minicab firms are already supply-capped on those days. For planned disruptions the useful output is **advance staffing**, which is a later add-on.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The customer is a small/mid London minicab operator. The value is winning riders priced out by Uber's surge, not "a better heatmap than Uber".
2. **The model considers every relevant factor** (user's requirement): unplanned disruptions, planned closures and strikes, weather, major events, time of day and day of week, and how they **interact**. Each factor type maps to a different action:
   - Unplanned disruption → reposition idle drivers **now**
   - Planned strike/works → **staff up in advance**
   - Weather / events → **amplify** both the baseline and the disruption uplift
3. The output is an **action** a dispatcher can take in minutes. The map is supporting context.
4. The model is transparent and factor-by-factor: every hotspot shows each factor's contribution in one plain-English line, and every number comes with a range. All parameters are labelled as published evidence or as assumptions.
5. The prototype goes back to the operator before the write-up claims a business case (1 operator required, 3 as the target).

### Phase A, week 1 (Sep 23 – Sep 30): the core loop only
1. **Logger (day 1):** poll the TfL `/Line/Mode/tube,elizabeth-line/Status` endpoint and the disruption endpoints every 60 s. In parallel, log **Santander BikePoint dock occupancy** (`/BikePoint`) every 5 min as the minute-level proxy. **Storage:** raw JSON is written **gzipped to the worker's local disk, only when the response changes** (compare a hash of the JSON with volatile timestamp fields such as `created`/`modified` stripped, otherwise every poll looks changed), so the parser can be re-run later. Parsed **disruptions** go to Supabase from day 1 (small). **BikePoint stays local** as parquet; the backtest is computed offline and only its small results table is uploaded (eng review T2). The logger is its **own process**, separate from the alerter/bot, auto-restarted by launchd/systemd, and it writes a heartbeat row; no poll for 10 min → Telegram ping to the builder (eng review 1A). **Backup:** a GitHub Actions job saves raw TfL snapshots every 5 min into the public repo; deduplicated later (eng review OV#9). **Start a ~20-line raw logger tonight**, before any other work.
2. **Disruption parser (budget 1–2 days):** turn each disruption into `{key, line, category, severity, affected_stations[], t0, is_unplanned, area_uncertain}`.
   - Use `affectedStops` / `affectedRoutes` where present. Otherwise match names in the description after **normalisation** (case, apostrophes, `&`/`and`, `St.`) plus an **alias table** grown from real logs (eng review 5A).
   - **Expand A–B ranges along TfL `/Line/{id}/Route/Sequence`**, branch-aware: "between Stockwell and Morden" → all 10 stations from Stockwell to Morden, not just the 2 endpoints. Golden tests cover Northern and District branch cases (OV#1).
   - **Map every TfL `statusSeverity` code** to a severity class, with an explicit default (OV#2).
   - **Line-wide** disruption (no section) → alert says "line-wide, no station suggestion" rather than ranking the busiest stations. **Zero name matches** on a sectional description → whole-line at half severity, `area_uncertain = true`, and a builder ping with the raw text (5A, OV#2).
   - **Incident key** is ours, not TfL's: `hash(line, parsed section)`. Severity is deliberately *not* in the key, so a minor → severe escalation is an **update** of one incident rather than a new alert (the lifecycle below requires this). `t0` = TfL's `validityPeriods.fromDate` when given, else the first poll that saw it (OV#3).
   - `is_unplanned` = TfL category `RealTime` vs `PlannedWork`.
3. **Engine v1:** baseline × disruption uplift only (the formula below, with weather and event multipliers fixed at 1).
4. **Telegram alert bot** to the dispatcher, with the alert lifecycle below.
5. Put it on the operator's phone by Sep 30.
6. **By Sep 30, count qualifying events** in the week-1 logs within the operator's area. If fewer than ~1/week, add Overground, DLR and National Rail (same TfL API). Agree a **replay demo** of a recorded disruption with the operator in advance (OV#4).

## Factor Engine (shared core)

**Stations in scope:** Tube + Elizabeth line stations from TfL `/StopPoint/Mode/tube,elizabeth-line` (about 300 points). No grid cells.

**Formula**, per station `s` at time `t`:

```
uplift(s,t) = Σ_d  severity(d) · exp(−dist(s, d) / λ) · g(t − t0_d; dur_d)     # disruption only, fraction
mult(s,t)   = weather_mult × event_mult(s)
risk(s,t)   = baseline(s, hour, dow) × (1 + uplift(s,t)) × mult(s,t)
pct(s,t)    = (1 + uplift) × mult − 1        # shown to the dispatcher, vs a normal day
extra(s,t)  = baseline × uplift × mult       # demand the DISRUPTION adds (weather/events amplify it): RANKING + car allocation
```

- `dist(s, d)` = minimum straight-line distance from `s` to any station in `d.affected_stations`. Stations inside the affected section count as distance 0.
- Weather and events multiply the **whole** expression, so rain *amplifies* the disruption spike. That's the interaction premise 2 requires.
- `baseline(s, t)` = **size × shape**, a labelled relative-busyness estimate (it does not measure taxi demand). **Size**: TfL annual station counts 2023 (typical daily entries + exits by day type) relative to the median station. **Shape**: TfL `/crowding` 15-min profiles, blended 50/50 with London's typical shape (shrinkage), or the typical shape alone where a station's profile is implausible (correlation < 0.6; e.g. Clapham Common's Friday) or missing (35 Elizabeth line stations, 16 all-zero Tube profiles). It makes a Friday-18:00 disruption at a busy station rank above the same disruption at 23:00 at a quiet one.
- **Why `extra` uses the disruption's uplift, not `pct`:** rain raises `pct` everywhere (+25%), so ranking by `baseline × pct` let big stations the disruption barely reaches (Victoria, Vauxhall) top a Northern line alert. Ranking by riders the disruption adds keeps rain as an amplifier, never a cause. A much busier station just outside the section can still rank: it genuinely gains more riders.
- **Service area:** in bot settings the dispatcher sets a base postcode and a radius (default 5 km). Only stations inside it are ranked or alerted, and alerts list the **top 3 by `extra`**.

**Initial parameter values**, stored once in `params.yaml` (value, unit, source label). The engine takes params as an argument; the console's "how this works" panel and the write-up's table are both rendered from the same file (eng review 4A). Every one is an assumption until the backtest:

| Parameter | Initial value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| severity: Suspended / Part suspended / Severe delays / Minor delays | 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.12 / 0.03 | Assumption, scaled so an unplanned suspension gives about +33% at affected stations in dry, normal conditions (≈ the ~+30% literature anchor) |
| unplanned multiplier on severity | ×1.3 (planned ×0.6: riders already re-routed) | Assumption |
| λ (spatial decay) | 0.8 km | Assumption: roughly one-stop walking radius |
| g: time profile | linear ramp over 8 min, plateau until `dur`, exponential decay (half-life 15 min) after | Assumption |
| dur (expected duration) when TfL gives none | default Minor 20 min, Severe 40, Part-suspended 60, Suspended 90. While active, re-estimated every poll as `dur = max(default, elapsed + default/2)` | Assumption |
| weather_mult | 1.0 dry; 1.1 light rain (<2 mm/h); 1.25 heavy rain (≥2 mm/h); +0.05 if <3 °C | Assumption |
| event_mult | 1.0; 1.15 if an event of ≥10k capacity ends within 1.5 km in the next 60 min; 1.3 if ≥40k | Assumption |

**Range (confidence), built on what can actually be counted:** every output shows a low–high band. The band width comes from (a) parameter uncertainty: **low** = engine run with severity ×0.5 and λ ×0.5, **high** = severity ×1.5 and λ ×1.5, **mid** = nominal, narrowed only once the backtest supports it, and (b) a **data-coverage count pooled by disruption category across all lines** ("{N} logged severe-delay events, all lines", with N read from the logs), not per line, because per-line counts would read "low" on almost everything before Oct 20 (OV#6). It never shows a count the logs can't support.

**Explanation:** one line built from the 2–3 largest contributions, e.g. "Northern line suspended (unplanned) + heavy rain + Friday peak."

**Check-ins in the model:** a check-in stores `{station, timestamp, status: busy|normal|dead, driver_id}`. For 30 min after a check-in, the hotspot shows it **alongside** the estimate as ground truth ("2 drivers report busy, 6 min ago"). A "dead" check-in suppresses repeat alerts for that station. Check-ins do **not** change the numeric estimate in v1. They are logged for validation.

## Alerts (Phase A)

**Recipient:** the dispatcher, via a Telegram bot. **Access:** the dispatcher's and drivers' Telegram chat IDs are listed in a local config file (never committed); all other chats are ignored (eng review 2A, trimmed). Driver IDs are stored hashed. Drivers use `/checkin`. Check-in reply buttons offer the **top 5 stations of the current alert** (no driver location needed).

**Lifecycle per incident key** (state persisted in a Supabase `alerts` table: key, state, last_sent_at, last_top3, telegram message_id; reloaded on start, so restarts are invisible and updates **edit the original message**; eng review 3A):
- **new:** fire when the disruption-only `uplift` (mid value, weather/events excluded) is ≥ 0.15 at any in-area station. Weather or events alone never trigger an alert (heavy rain alone = +25% `pct`). One combined message if several disruptions start within the same 5-min window.
- **update:** re-send only if severity changes category, or the top-3 stations change. There's a 10-min cooldown per disruption otherwise, which absorbs status flapping. A disruption that clears and returns within 10 min is treated as the same event.
- **resolved:** one short "✅ Northern line back to good service, 18:52" message.
- Quiet hours are configurable (default off).

**Action sizing:** the dispatcher sets `idle_drivers` (how many cars are usually free at that time) once, in bot settings.
**Per alert** (not per station): `cars = clamp(round(0.5 × pct_mid × idle_drivers), 1, idle_drivers)`, where `pct_mid` is the mean mid-value `pct` of the top-3 stations (as a fraction). The cars are split across the 3 stations in proportion to `extra`. If `idle_drivers` isn't set, the alert says "raise coverage" with no number. `0.5` is an assumption, tuned through operator feedback.

**Worked example** (computed from the formula and table above: all 10 stations from Stockwell to Morden are inside the suspended section so dist = 0, and the top 3 are picked by `extra`, i.e. by baseline busyness (station names below are illustrative until the baseline is built); uplift = 0.25 × 1.3 = 0.325 (low 0.163, high 0.488); heavy rain ×1.25 → pct = +66% (+45% to +86%); cars = round(0.5 × 0.656 × 6) = 2; dur defaults to 90 min from 18:07):
> ⚠️ Northern line suspended Stockwell–Morden (unplanned, 18:07). Heavy rain, Friday peak.
> Expect **+45–86%** booking demand around **Stockwell, Clapham North, Clapham South** until at least ~19:35 (no TfL estimate yet).
> Suggest: **move 2 of your 6 idle cars** to wait nearby (not at the station) for fast pickups. Based on {N} logged unplanned suspensions, all lines.

### What already exists
Nothing in `Surge/` reuses existing code. Questly (the parent repo) shares no modules.
